MIRAGE

Survey the basin with five companions. Every one of you carries a meter you cannot see, and it only counts down. At zero, a mind starts inventing the world — theirs, or yours.

Goal. Find and survey all six markers, then get back to camp with at least two companions still walking with you.

The meters are invisible. Nobody has a bar. You read your party instead: who lags, who goes quiet, who starts narrating a ridge that isn't moving. Ask them — but a fraying companion shades the truth and one who is gone will tell you they're fine.

Pylons are the only renewable relief. Stand inside one and everybody in range comes back up; they run down while in use and recharge while left alone. Three lumen doses exist for six people.

When you go. The screen stops being a witness — markers appear where there are none, a sixth companion joins the formation, spent pylons glow like full ones, north moves. Logging a marker with nobody lucid beside you can write down something that was never there.

Items turn up in the fog too — a flare, a tether, a lens. What the item bar SAYS an item is may not be what it truly is while you're gone, and something you pick up while hallucinating might not be there at all. Either way it can be carried and used — you only find out which kind of surprise it was the moment you use it.

Crafting. Carrying two combinable items and pressing craft fuses them into something stronger — an Ember, a Beacon, or a Ward, each doing both of its parts' work at once. Crafting reads what you're ACTUALLY carrying, not what the item bar told you, so a hallucinating lead can reach for a pair that doesn't combine — or doesn't exist.

The basin isn't alone. Clearing one survey pushes the party into the next, harder basin — carrying doses, items, and every scar with them. The campaign ends when you fail one, or clear the last.
